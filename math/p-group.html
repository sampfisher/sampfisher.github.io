The mod \(p\) group algebra of a \(p\)-group

Let \(G\) be a finite \(p\)-group. The goal of this post is to prove classical facts about the group algebra \(\mathbb{F}_p[G]\): the augmentation ideal of \(\mathbb F_p[G]\) is nilpotent and it is the unique maximal ideal (so \(\mathbb F_p[G]\) is a local ring). We will actually allow the coefficient ring to be slightly more general than \(\mathbb F_p\) in what follows.

The augmentation ideal

Let \(R\) be a ring and let \(G\) be a group. The augmentation homomorphism of the group ring \(R[G]\) is the ring homomorphism \[ \alpha_{R[G]} \colon R[G] \to R, \qquad \sum_{g \in G} r_g g \mapsto \sum_{g \in G} r_g. \] Here, \(r_g \in R\) for all \(g \in G\) and all but finitely many \(r_g\)'s are non-zero. The augmentation ideal \(\omega_{R[G]}\) of \(R[G]\) is the kernel of \(\alpha_{R[G]}\). It plays a fundamental role in the study of \(R[G]\) and the group cohomology of \(G\).
