The Borel Density Theorem

In a reading group at ICMAT on arithmetic groups (organised by Macarena Arenas) I spoke about the Borel Density Theorem. The post covers what I talked about, and is based on chapter 4 of Dave Witte Morris's book Introduction to Arithmetic Groups. The main result we will prove is the following.

Borel Density Theorem. Let \(G\) be a connected semisimple Lie group with no compact factors. If \(\Gamma < G\) is a lattice, then \(\Gamma\) is Zariski dense in \(G\).

Throughout the post \(G\) will always denote a connected semisimple Lie group without compact factors and \(\Gamma\) will always be a lattice. Recall that a Lie group is semisimple if it decomposes as a direct product of simple Lie groups, where a Lie group is simple if it is not Abelian and it has no non-trivial, normal, closed, connected, proper subgroups. Moreover, following Witte Morris, we assume that \(G \subseteq \mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb R)\) for some \(n \in \mathbb N\), i.e. Lie groups are by assumption linear.

The Borel Density Theorem will be a consequence of the following result.

Theorem 1. Let \(V\) be a real or complex vector space, and let \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_m(V)\) be a continuous representation of \(G\) on \(V\).

  1. If \(v \in V\) is fixed by \(\Gamma\), then it is fixed by \(G\).
  2. If \(W \subseteq V\) is fixed (set-wise) by \(\Gamma\), then it is fixed by \(G\).

The main goal of this post will be to prove Theorem 1. After that we will quickly derive some nice consequences.

1. Some basic facts about lattices in Lie groups

Recall, \(\Gamma\) denotes a lattice in the connected semisimple Lie group \(G\) (which has no compact factors). An element of \(u \in G\) is unipotent if there exists \(n \in \mathbb N\) such that \((u - 1)^n = 0\).

Fact 2. \(G\) is generated by its unipotent elements.
